Scientific publication is an important milestone in academic work. I added these resources because AI is rapidly changing the landscape of the physical sciences, including how we prepare, review and publish research. If you are a student or reader feeling unsure about how to navigate these changes, these journal guidelines can serve as an important starting point.

Policies differ between journals and will continue to evolve. Always consult your intended journal's latest policy before submitting a manuscript.
{: .callout}

## American Astronomical Society (AAS) journals

[New Guidelines on AI Use in AAS Journals](https://journals.aas.org/news/new-guidelines-on-ai-use-in-aas-journals/)

An introduction to the AAS guidance for authors and reviewers, covering manuscript preparation, disclosure of AI use and the confidentiality of peer review. The accompanying [author guidelines](https://journals.aas.org/author-llm-guidelines) and [reviewer guidelines](https://journals.aas.org/referee-llm-guidelines) give the detailed requirements and examples.

## Astronomy & Astrophysics (A&A)

[A&A publishes statement on the use of AI-assisted technologies](https://www.aanda.org/news/3200-a-a-publishes-statement-on-the-use-of-ai-assisted-technologies)

The journal's statement on AI-assisted technologies in scientific publishing. Read it when considering AI use in a manuscript intended for A&A.

## American Physical Society (APS) journals

[Appropriate Use of AI Tools](https://journals.aps.org/authors/appropriate-use-ai-tools)

Guidance for authors, reviewers and editors on human responsibility, disclosure of substantive AI use, verification of AI-assisted content and protection of confidential material. Its FAQ explains what counts as substantive use and where disclosures belong in a paper.

## How to use these resources

Start with the policy of the journal you plan to submit to. Check which uses of AI are permitted, what must be disclosed and how you should verify the output. Requirements for language editing, drafting, coding, figures and peer review can differ. Keep the original journal guidance as your reference as the landscape changes.
