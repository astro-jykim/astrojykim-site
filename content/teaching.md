I teach physics and astrophysics at UNIST, from first-year general physics to advanced astrophysics, and before that taught astronomy courses at Kyungpook National University (KNU). Research students in the group often start from these courses. Syllabi are available on request.

## Courses at UNIST

**General Physics I** — first-year undergraduate (Spring 2026)
: The mandatory general physics course for first-year students.

**Computational Physics** — third-year undergraduate (Spring 2025, Spring 2026)
: Numerical and computational methods for physics problems: linear and nonlinear equations, ordinary and partial differential equations, Monte Carlo methods, optimization, and machine learning and deep learning.

**Astrophysics: Galaxies and the Universe** — fourth-year undergraduate (Fall 2024, Fall 2025)
: An introduction to galactic and extragalactic astronomy and cosmology.

## Courses at Kyungpook National University (2022–2024)

**Radio Astronomy / Advanced Observational Astronomy** — graduate (2022, 2024)
: From the fundamentals of radiation measurements and radio-frequency electronics to interferometry, aperture synthesis and the analysis of real interferometer data.

**High Energy Astrophysics** — graduate (2023)
: The basics of the high-energy astrophysics of compact objects, and recent theoretical and observational results through intensive reading and review of the literature.

**Advanced Computational Astrophysics** — graduate (2023, five weeks)
: Statistical and machine-learning methods adapted for students' own research.

**Individual Astronomy Research** — graduate (2024)
: Individual research in close interaction with the supervisor.

**Astronomical Big Data Science / Astronomical Image Processing** — fourth-year undergraduate (2022–2023)
: Statistical analysis and machine-learning techniques and their application to astronomy, in theory and practice.

**Astronomical Observations and Practice 2** — third-year undergraduate (2022–2023)
: Optical/infrared spectroscopy and multi-wavelength observations, covering facilities from radio to gamma rays, with hands-on analysis of the LAB 21-cm Galactic HI survey and ALMA science-verification data. The [2.3 m radio telescope](/2-3m-radio-telescope/) installed at KNU in 2023 was also used for education.

**Understanding the Universe** — liberal-arts course for students of all majors (2022–2023)
